The intercompany work is the real job. Each file carries a due-to and due-from line for every other file, and today those pairs do not perfectly mirror. We reconcile them entity by entity and eliminate them at the group level, so the roll-up you and Forrest look at actually holds together instead of quietly carrying a difference. Where a pair will not tie, you hear about it from us with the number attached, not later from a diligence team.

August 14, the day that cannot move

Every month, eleven separate sales tax filings come due on one date. It is the single heaviest day in your cycle, so we plan the whole month backwards from it and stack nothing else on top of it.

11
separate returns filed every month, one per operating entity
8.25%
the rate at most shops. Kingsland and the roastery file at 6.25%
0.5%
timely-filing discount, kept by filing monthly and never late
4
revenue sources reconciled per shop before a return is prepared

Preparing a Mojo return is not a single number off a report. Sales come in through the point of sale and three delivery platforms, and cash is rung tax-inclusive, so the tax has to be backed out of it before anything is taxable. Every shop gets its own workpaper, every workpaper gets reviewed, and only then does a return get filed.

You will see the same package every month: the per-entity workpaper showing how the number was built, and the filed confirmation. If we ever disagree with how something was treated historically, you get the reasoning in writing before we change it.

One thing we want to close early. Some shops carry beer and wine income. Texas treats alcohol differently depending on the permit a location holds, and we want to confirm the permit type at those shops rather than assume the historical treatment was right. It is a short conversation and we would rather have it now than at a filing deadline.

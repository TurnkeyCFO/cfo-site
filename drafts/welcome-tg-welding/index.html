What we fix first, and why it matters to you

Four structural things in the books are worth naming plainly, because each one changes a number you actually use to run the shop. This is the month-one work.

01

Materials move into cost of goods sold

Steel and consumables are currently sitting in general expenses rather than in cost of goods sold, alongside your equipment rental and freight.

What changes for you

Gross margin starts reading true. Right now a job's profit is buried. Once material lands where it belongs, you can see what a tether plate run actually makes versus a gate install, and price the next one from evidence.

02

The unassigned spend gets a name

Roughly one hundred twenty-six thousand dollars of spend over the trailing year has no vendor attached to it. It is real money, it just has no label.

What changes for you

You get a real supplier picture. Which steel yard you actually spend the most with, what your gas is really costing, and where there is leverage on terms. You cannot negotiate against a number you cannot see.

03

Your invoicing system and QuickBooks get one agreed relationship

Your invoices and receivables live in the invoicing app we built. QuickBooks currently shows almost nothing in receivables, so the two tell different stories about what is owed to you.

What changes for you

One number for what you are owed. We will recommend how the two should relate, you decide, and from then on the outstanding figure is the same whichever one you open.

04

A closed-books date goes on the file

The books are reconciled through June, but nothing is locked, so a prior month can still move underneath you without anyone noticing.

What changes for you

History stays put. Once a month is closed and locked it stops changing, which is what makes the trend line worth reading and keeps your CPA from re-deriving last year in September.
